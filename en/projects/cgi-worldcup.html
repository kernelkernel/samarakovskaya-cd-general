One tournament, two brands.

CGI · FOOH
Final · 19 July 2026

A month when the whole world watches one story.

The World Cup is the largest sporting event on the planet. For a month, billions of people follow a single storyline in real time. Any story that enters that orbit becomes impossible to ignore.

Both concepts are built on the same logic: the idea grows out of a brand asset instead of being stapled to the occasion with a sponsor logo. For adidas that asset is a trophy that officially carries the brand name. For Powerade it is the status of official drink of the tournament, plus the one subject everyone is already discussing: the heat.

Both run in a single format: FOOH. Hyperreal CGI shot in the logic of accidental phone footage, made for a vertical feed rather than for the street. There is almost always a bystander with a phone in frame: the viewer sees the event exactly as it will appear in the feed, and the first instinct is to check whether it is real.

Concept 01

adidas · Golden Boot.

ONE WIN. BIGGER THAN ONE.
A personal award becomes a national one

The right to the name

adidas makes TRIONDA, the official match ball, kits for dozens of national teams, works with key players and has been a FIFA partner for more than 50 years. The decisive detail: the award for the tournament’s top scorer is officially called the adidas Golden Boot. The brand is not borrowing a news hook — it owns the object that carries its name.

Award since 1982 11 winners 50+ years with FIFA

The moment the brand gives away

The race for the Golden Boot is one of the tournament’s live dramas. But at the final whistle attention drifts away from the stadium, the presentation stays a small moment on the podium, and the brand turns up hours later with a congratulatory post. adidas pays for the win and then steps into the background at the exact moment it happens.

TOO BIG FOR ONE

The moment the top scorer is confirmed, three heavy-lift helicopters deliver a giant golden boot to the main square of the winner’s capital. The image reads without explanation and is ready to become news the same minute.

Money shot

The boot hangs a metre above the plinth. The winner stands beneath it, back to the camera, with the capital, the crowd and a forest of raised phones all around. The frame is not a finished monument, it is the second before contact. That is where the tension lives. A single shot works at once as a film, a cover, a news picture and an outdoor poster.

Three endings, prepared in advance

A week before the final, the three teams leading the race are selected. An empty plinth appears in London, Paris and Buenos Aires, carrying the line ONE OF THESE WILL BE FILLED TONIGHT, the object still hidden under a gold cover. A crowd gathers around something that is not there yet. Once the result is in, two plinths stay empty and one is filled: all three cities are produced in parallel, and the right film goes live the minute the name is announced.

Concept 02

Powerade · Empower State.

THE WORLD’S BIGGEST COOLER
The final in New York

Heat as the tournament’s own storyline

A heat dome over the host cities and a cooling break in every match. Hydration has stopped being a quiet moment on the touchline: players need the pause, fans feel it break the rhythm of the game. Powerade can flip how that moment is read and carry the break off the pitch, into the overheated city. From a break in the game to a break from the heat.

The right to the subject

Powerade is the official sports drink of the FIFA World Cup 2026. If the whole world is talking about how to survive the heat, the brand has to answer at the scale of the event itself. The final is played in New York, which puts one of the planet’s most recognisable landmarks within reach.

EMPOWER STATE BUILDING

On the day of the final, the Empire State Building starts to freeze over. Ice spreads across the façade, a giant section opens and reveals thousands of ice-cold bottles bursting out into the Manhattan heat. The city’s landmark stops being scenery and becomes the idea itself. Empire turns into Empower: one letter changes the meaning.

Shot like accidental footage

Hyperreal CGI built on the logic of phone footage: photoreal frost, vapour, live camera movement. The hero film is not released first — it lands as the answer to a run of cryptic clips. The same impossible event seen from different streets, rooftops and phones, rolled out in waves rather than a single drop.

The on-screen event turns physical

At the final, the pitch itself becomes the surface of a cooler: the brand name is cut into the grass and reaches every broadcast camera. Across the host cities, bus stops ice over and turn into working coolers you can step into and take a cold drink from. New York takes a three-minute cooling break, all at the same moment. Screens in fan zones, bars and arenas frost over simultaneously. The cold travels from city to city, following the tournament.

Method

Why it works.

The formula
for viral CGI

Brand truth instead of a sponsor sticker

The idea grows out of an asset the brand already owns. An award carrying the adidas name. Powerade’s status as the official drink. No competitor can hijack that link, and the client never has to explain why it is the one speaking.

One impossible image

Helicopters, a golden boot and the main square. A freezing skyscraper and an avalanche of bottles. The frame reads in a second, with no copy and no context, in any language.

Credibility instead of a special effect

Phone angles, dust, rigging cables, frost on the asphalt. It reads as documentary evidence, not as a render. The argument over whether it is real is the sharing mechanic.

Timing instead of budget

The image lands together with the news, not a few hours behind it. The formula: a live result, an impossible scale, local pride and credible evidence.
